# MASTER PROJECT DIRECTIVE
## Redesign of Mujahid's Personal Website

Website: https://mujahidmahfuz.github.io/mujahid-s-persona/

Repository: https://github.com/mujahidmahfuz/mujahid-s-persona

Your task is to transform this website into an extraordinary personal digital experience.

This is not a conventional portfolio redesign.

It is the creation of a distinctive digital world that represents a human being's intellectual curiosity, creativity, ambitions, experiences, research, imperfections, and ongoing journey.

The website must feel beautiful, meaningful, immersive, intelligent, personal, and timeless.

Read this entire document before implementation.

---

# 1. THE CENTRAL VISION

Working creative concept:

"An Atlas of a Curious Mind."

Think of a combination of:

- An old astronomical observatory.
- A forgotten library.
- An inventor's personal journal.
- An antique scientific atlas.
- A collection of handwritten discoveries.
- A contemporary editorial publication.
- A window into an unfamiliar universe.
- A personal archive that continues to evolve.

However, do not simply reproduce these aesthetics.

Develop an original visual identity from them.

The atmosphere should communicate:

- Wonder.
- Intellectual curiosity.
- Humanity.
- Nostalgia.
- Discovery.
- Ambition.
- Tranquility.
- A little melancholy.
- A sense of infinite possibility.

The experience should have sweetness with a little bittersweetness.

It should feel old in its soul but contemporary in its execution.

IMPORTANT:

Do not turn the website into a stereotypical space-themed portfolio.

Do not fill it with planets, stars, galaxies, glowing particles, and random astronomical illustrations.

The universe is a metaphor for exploration, possibility, and the human mind.

Use it intelligently and selectively.

The website must have a personality, not merely an aesthetic.

# 2. THE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Follow these principles throughout the entire website.

### Principle A — Meaning before decoration

Every significant visual element must have a purpose.

Animations should communicate something.

Illustrations should help people understand something.

Typography should establish personality.

Colours should create emotion and identity.

Never add visual effects merely because they look fashionable.

### Principle B — Beauty through restraint

Use sophisticated compositions, generous spacing, thoughtful typography, and carefully positioned visual elements.

Do not overload the visitor.

Allow important content to breathe.

A simple composition can be extraordinary when its proportions, colours, typography, and details are right.

### Principle C — Discovery instead of information dumping

The website should encourage exploration.

Do not display every achievement, project, statistic, and publication immediately.

Organize information so visitors can gradually discover the person and the work.

However, all important information must remain accessible.

### Principle D — Every page should feel considered

A page should never feel like an automatically generated database entry.

Each page must have an intentional composition, personality, introductory reflection, and appropriate visual treatment.

Maintain a consistent design language without making every page visually identical.

# 3. COLOUR — A NON-NEGOTIABLE REQUIREMENT

Do not use a conventional black-white-gray design system.

Do not make a monochromatic website and call it sophisticated.

Colour must be a fundamental part of the visual identity.

Explore a rich, carefully balanced chromatic palette.

Suggested colour families:

- Aged parchment and warm ivory.
- Deep verdigris and botanical green.
- Ultramarine and historical cobalt.
- Muted burgundy and mulberry.
- Burnt terracotta and copper.
- Saffron and antique amber.
- Moss and olive.
- Deep petrol and blue-green.

Possible starting references:

| Warm parchment | #F1E4C9 |

These are exploratory starting points, not a rigid final palette.

You must develop a complete colour system with:

- Primary surfaces.
- Secondary surfaces.
- Accent colours.
- Text colours.
- Borders.
- Interaction states.
- Highlight colours.
- Dark and light section combinations.

Use colour intentionally.

Different research disciplines and projects may have their own subtle colour identities while remaining part of the same visual universe.

For example, a biological research project could use botanical greens and warm copper, while quantum research could use indigo and verdigris.

Do not apply these mappings mechanically.

Avoid excessive gradients, artificial neon, generic glassmorphism, and fashionable colour combinations without character.

Black, white, and gray may appear when functionally necessary, but must not dominate the identity.

Check every important text/background combination for WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.

# 4. TYPOGRAPHY

Create a sophisticated typographic system.

Use three complementary directions:

1. Editorial serif:
   Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond, Literata, or an appropriate alternative.

2. Contemporary sans-serif:
   Inter, Manrope, or another clean readable typeface.

3. Technical typography:
   IBM Plex Mono or JetBrains Mono.

For Quranic Arabic, use an appropriate Arabic typeface such as Amiri or Noto Naskh Arabic.

Use the serif for emotional expressions, page titles, quotations, and editorial moments.

Use the sans-serif for readable content and navigation.

Use monospace for metadata, publication information, coordinates, dates, technical labels, and research identifiers.

Typography must feel deliberately composed.

Avoid excessively large headings everywhere.

Avoid making every section use the same heading format.

# 5. THE MOST IMPORTANT ELEMENT: A HUMAN THOUGHT ON EVERY PAGE

Every page must have its own dedicated epigraph.

This is a mandatory site-wide feature.

An epigraph can be:

- A verse from the Quran.
- A philosophical reflection.
- A literary quotation.
- A line from poetry.
- A historical thought.
- An original personal reflection.

The purpose is to establish an emotional connection between the visitor and the subject of the page.

These words should encourage reflection about:

- Life.
- The meaning of living.
- Knowledge.
- Creation.
- Time.
- Human limitations.
- Curiosity.
- Patience.
- Failure.
- Hope.
- Responsibility.
- Uncertainty.
- Discovery.
- The relationship between humanity and the universe.

### Quranic references

Treat Quranic verses with respect and accuracy.

When using a Quranic verse:

- Include the original Arabic where appropriate.
- Include the Surah name and verse number.
- Provide the English translation.
- Identify the translation source.
- Preserve its original meaning and context.
- Never fabricate a verse or attribution.
- Never alter a verse to make it aesthetically convenient.
- Avoid extracting a verse in a way that misrepresents its message.

Use a reliable translation and record the source.

### Other quotations

Every quotation must have a verifiable attribution.

If the thought is original, explicitly identify it as an original reflection.

Do not invent famous quotations or attribute original writing to historical figures.

### Epigraph design

Create a reusable but visually sophisticated component.

Its composition may change depending on the page.

Possible treatments:

- A large editorial quotation.
- A small inscription in the margin.
- A verse accompanied by delicate Arabic typography.
- A handwritten-style reflection.
- A short sentence printed like a note in an old journal.
- A quotation integrated into a scientific illustration.

Do not make every epigraph look like the same quotation card.

Do not use generic inspirational quotes.

The words must relate meaningfully to the page.

### Page coverage

Every individual route must have a dedicated epigraph.

This includes:

- Homepage.
- About.
- Research index.
- Individual research pages.
- Publications.
- Individual project pages.
- Achievements.
- Sports.
- Education.
- Experience.
- Notes.
- Contact.
- CV.
- Search.
- 404 page.
- Other existing or future routes.

Research pages should receive reflections connected to knowledge, evidence, understanding, and discovery.

Project pages should receive reflections connected to creation, imagination, experimentation, and responsibility.

Sports pages should receive reflections connected to discipline, failure, perseverance, and teamwork.

About pages should be more personal and philosophical.

Maintain an epigraph registry or structured content system.

Avoid accidental repetition.

# 7. RESEARCH PAGES — EVERY PAPER DESERVES ITS OWN EXPERIENCE

Research is one of the most important parts of this website.

Do not treat research pages as ordinary article templates.

Each individual paper must have its own dedicated visual explanation and animation.

The animation must communicate the actual research contribution.

### Every paper page should include

- Dedicated epigraph.
- Paper title.
- Publication status.
- Journal or conference name.
- Authors.
- Publication date.
- DOI.
- Journal cover or publication imagery.
- PDF access.
- Plain-language explanation.
- Research problem.
- Methodology.
- Mathematical formulation where relevant.
- Interactive explanation.
- Results.
- Figures and visualizations.
- Limitations.
- Personal contribution.
- Citation.
- Related research.

### Signature animation per paper

Create an animation specifically for the paper.

Do not simply reuse one generic animation across every research page.

Examples:

For an explainable AI paper:
Show how multiple attribution explanations disagree, how aggregation works, and how the proposed method produces a more stable consensus.

For quantum computing:
Animate the relevant quantum states, circuits, operations, or physical processes where these help explain the method.

For continual learning:
Show the progression of learning, memory consolidation, replay, and forgetting.

These examples are illustrative. The actual animation must follow the actual paper.

### Research visualization requirements

Animations must be technically honest.

Distinguish clearly between:

- Actual experimental results.
- Illustrative examples.
- Simulated data.
- Conceptual diagrams.

Never fabricate experimental data.

Never create scientific animations that visually imply results the paper does not establish.

If a visualization uses toy numbers, identify them as illustrative.

Provide controls where appropriate:

- Play.
- Pause.
- Reset.
- Previous step.
- Next step.
- Explanation of the current step.

Every animation must have a static fallback.

Use 2D explanations as the default.

Use 3D when it genuinely improves understanding.

A beautiful but scientifically misleading animation is unacceptable.

# 9. PUBLICATIONS — CREATE A LIVING RESEARCH LIBRARY

This section deserves exceptional attention.

The publications page should feel like entering a collection of beautifully preserved academic works.

It must not resemble a conventional list of DOI links.

### Journal covers and front pages

For every published journal article, provide appropriate publication imagery.

Image priority:

1. Actual official journal issue cover, when available and relevant.
2. Actual article first page or title page.
3. An original editorial cover composition inspired by the paper, when official imagery is unavailable.

Verify that the image corresponds to the correct publication and issue.

Do not use a journal logo as a substitute for an actual journal cover.

Do not fabricate an official journal cover.

Clearly distinguish original artwork from official publisher imagery.

Check reuse permissions and provide appropriate attribution.

### Publication card design

Create a sophisticated paper-like card component.

Each card should visually resemble a physical academic paper or carefully preserved journal issue.

Consider:

- An actual cover or front-page image.
- Paper texture.
- Layered paper edges.
- Subtle shadows.
- Fine borders.
- A small publication stamp.
- Journal name.
- Volume and issue information where available.
- Publication year.
- Paper title.
- Authors.
- DOI.
- Publication status.

### Card interaction

Cards should feel physical and interactive.

On desktop:

- Gentle perspective movement.
- Subtle lifting.
- Small rotation.
- Layered depth.
- Controlled shadow changes.
- Smooth image transitions.

On interaction:

- Reveal additional publication metadata.
- Provide access to the paper page.
- Provide a separate PDF action.
- Provide DOI and publisher links.

The card must look beautiful without animation too.

Do not hide important information behind hover states.

On mobile, provide appropriate tap interactions.

Avoid excessive 3D effects or gimmicky page-turn animations.

The goal is to make academic publications feel like meaningful objects rather than database entries.

# 10. PDF AND PAPER EXPERIENCE

- Read paper.

# 12. MOTION AND INTERACTION DESIGN

The site must combine static beauty with meaningful dynamic behaviour.

Develop a coherent motion system.

Possible interactions:

- Delicate page transitions.
- Scroll-based reveals.
- Layered paper movement.
- Interactive illustrations.
- Subtle orbital motion.
- Animated diagrams.
- Image transitions.
- Expandable research explanations.
- Small typographic movements.
- Contextual hover states.

Motion should have different levels of intensity.

Ordinary navigation should remain quiet.

Research explainers may be more interactive.

Project demonstrations may involve more complex movement.

Never animate everything simultaneously.

Avoid:

- Excessive particles.
- Random floating objects.
- Constant background movement.
- Cursor trails.
- Unnecessary parallax.
- Scroll hijacking.
- Flashy transitions.
- Autoplaying sound.
- Autoplaying video.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

All essential information must remain available without animation.

Pause expensive animations when their elements are outside the viewport or the browser tab is hidden.

# 13. NAVIGATION AND INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Visitors should always understand:

- Where they are.
- What they are looking at.
- How they arrived there.

The design should encourage discovery without confusing people.

# 16. ART DIRECTION QUALITY CONTROL

1. Does this page feel like part of a distinctive universe?
2. Does it have its own personality?
3. Is colour being used intentionally?
4. Does the typography feel carefully composed?
5. Does the page communicate something meaningful?
6. Is the epigraph relevant to the page?
7. Are animations useful rather than decorative?
8. Does the page feel personal rather than commercially templated?
9. Is the visual hierarchy clear?
10. Does it work beautifully on mobile?
11. Does it remain excellent without animation?
12. Would someone remember this experience after leaving?

If a page looks like a generic portfolio template, it is not finished.

If it looks like a collection of unrelated design experiments, it is not finished.

The entire website must feel like one coherent world.

A few additional implementation decisions I strongly recommend
There are three things I would pay particular attention to while Codex implements this.
1. Build a proper epigraph system
Don't hardcode quotations separately into every page.

Create a structured content system, for example:
interface Epigraph {
  text: string;
  type: "quran" | "literature" | "philosophy" | "poetry" | "original";
  attribution?: string;
  source?: string;
  surah?: string;
  verse?: string;
  arabic?: string;
  translation?: string;
  translationSource?: string;
}

This makes it possible to manage the quotations, verify their sources, prevent repetition, and give each page its own presentation.
2. Give every paper a visual identity
I particularly want to emphasize this.

My published paper should not just appear as a title inside a card.

Imagine opening the Publications section and seeing actual journal covers, article title pages, and beautifully composed paper objects.

For example, your Knowledge-Based Systems publication should have its actual publication imagery where obtainable, its bibliographic information, and a visually interesting card.

Clicking it should lead to a dedicated research experience containing its own scientific animation.

The same principle applies to your other publications.
3. Don't let yourself compromise the artistic direction
You can produce technically correct websites that look extremely ordinary.

It needs to be evaluated visually, not just through successful builds.

After every major phase, inspect the actual rendered website at desktop and mobile sizes.

A passing build does not mean a successful design.
My recommendation: Make docs/DESIGN.md the permanent source of truth for the new identity and CLAUDE.md the instruction to follow it. That way, future Claude sessions can add papers, projects, and pages without gradually destroying the visual character.

The website should evolve, but its soul should remain recognizable.
