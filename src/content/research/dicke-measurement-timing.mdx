## The problem

Dicke states are useful resources for quantum metrology and distributed quantum computing, but preparing them is hard when not every qubit can be reached directly. Thapa et al. gave a 24-gate circuit that expands a four-qubit Dicke state into a five-qubit one using two helper qubits, while one data qubit stays completely inaccessible. A "flag" helper qubit is measured only once, at the very end, to learn whether the expansion worked.

This paper asks whether the flag already knows the answer earlier — and if so, whether measuring it sooner saves cost without disturbing the successful result.

## What I found

- **The flag knows early.** Tracking the mutual information between the flag qubit and the final outcome shows it becomes informative well before the last gate.
- **A rule for when early is safe.** The *last-gate-targeting rule* says when measuring early leaves the successful output exactly unchanged; for this circuit the earliest safe point is gate 21 of 24.
- **It generalises.** The same rule holds on a second, structurally unrelated flag-qubit protocol (a W-state fusion scheme), checked at the size tested.
- **The hand-made circuit is efficient.** An exact alternative built by generic isometry synthesis — verified to fidelity one — needs more resources than the hand-designed circuit.

All results come from exact statevector simulation, cross-checked against a real superconducting device’s noise model, in an openly reproducible pipeline.

## What did not hold up

- **The “free” fidelity bonus.** Once the quality of the recycled failure-branch state is counted honestly, an earlier claim that early measurement improves fidelity at no cost disappears. On a three-objective Pareto frontier, the standard protocol is itself optimal.
- **The size of the saving.** About 3% fewer gates in the abstract cost model shrinks to about 1% once a real device’s two-qubit gate counts are used.
- **The published baseline.** My simulation of the reference circuit does not reproduce the published value. A single-gate change reproduces it exactly but contradicts the reference circuit’s own documentation, so it is reported as an open finding rather than adopted silently.
- **Weak values.** A genuine weak-value calculation for the flag qubit shows no anomalous behaviour.
