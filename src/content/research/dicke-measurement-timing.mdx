## The problem

Dicke states are useful resources for quantum metrology and distributed quantum computing, but preparing them is hard when not every qubit can be reached directly. Thapa et al. gave a 24-gate circuit that expands a four-qubit Dicke state into a five-qubit one using two helper qubits, while one data qubit stays completely inaccessible. A "flag" helper qubit is measured only once, at the very end, to learn whether the expansion worked.

This paper asks whether the flag already knows the answer earlier — and if so, whether measuring it sooner saves cost without disturbing the successful result.

All results come from exact statevector simulation, cross-checked against a real superconducting device's noise model. The paper also reports what did **not** hold up: once the recycled failure-branch state is counted honestly, an earlier claim of a free fidelity bonus disappears, and on a three-objective Pareto frontier the standard protocol is itself optimal. The paper's own simulation baseline for the reference circuit differs from the published value; a single-gate anomaly that reproduces the published number is reported as an open finding rather than adopted. A generic exact alternative built by isometry synthesis turns out less efficient than the hand-designed circuit, and a weak-value calculation for the flag qubit shows no anomalous behaviour.
