## The problem

When a neural network learns tasks one after another, training on a new task tends to overwrite what it learned before. This is called catastrophic forgetting. This project borrows an idea from biology — sleep, during which the brain replays and consolidates memories — and asks when a network should “sleep” so that it forgets less without wasting effort.

## Method

The work proceeds in three phases:

1. **Fixed-interval sleep** — a baseline that sleeps on a fixed schedule.
2. **Heuristic gate** — sleep is triggered by a rule based on the network's state.
3. **Learned policy** — a contextual bandit trained with REINFORCE decides when to sleep.

On Split CIFAR-10, sleep comes in graduated tiers — micro, light and deep — rather than a single kind.
