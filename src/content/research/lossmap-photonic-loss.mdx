Photonic quantum computers lose photons, and loss is their main way of failing. The usual fault-tolerance thresholds assume that loss is the same everywhere on the chip and does not change. Real hardware is not like that. Lossmap asks what a processor gains if it keeps track of where, and when, it is losing photons.

## The problem

Existing thresholds generally assume photon loss is **uniform** and **static**. In practice it is:

- **spatially heterogeneous** — different components lose photons at different rates, and
- **temporally dynamic** — loss drifts over time.

The research question: can a photonic quantum computer that continuously infers and forecasts its own loss landscape tolerate higher photon loss than one that assumes a uniform loss model?

## Method

The project is simulation-first, laptop-scale and validated step by step, in three stages:

In photonic quantum computing, whether a large resource state stays connected as links are lost is a percolation question. Before using the simulation engine on realistic loss maps, experiment **E001** checked it on a case with a known answer — bond percolation on a square lattice, whose threshold is exactly 0.5.
