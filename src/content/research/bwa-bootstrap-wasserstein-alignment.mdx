## The problem

Feature-attribution methods such as SHAP explain a model's prediction by assigning each input feature a share of it. On small datasets, these attributions can be unstable: a model trained on a slightly different sample of the data may yield a noticeably different explanation. BWA targets stable feature attribution in this low-data regime.

## Method

BWA works in three steps:

1. Draw bootstrap replicates of the training data and fit the model on each.
2. Compute a SHAP attribution vector for each replicate.
3. Combine the replicate attributions through an **entropic Wasserstein barycenter**, giving one aligned attribution.

The paper describes two variants:

- **BWA-v1** — a hard binomial sign test with global normalisation.
- **BWA-v2** — a magnitude-weighted Bayesian soft sign with per-feature median-absolute scaling.

For reference, the standard definition of an entropic Wasserstein barycenter of distributions <Math tex="\nu_1,\dots,\nu_K" /> with weights <Math tex="\lambda_k" /> is

<Math display tex="\bar\mu = \arg\min_{\mu} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \lambda_k \, W_\varepsilon(\mu, \nu_k)" />

where <Math tex="W_\varepsilon" /> is the entropy-regularised optimal-transport cost with regularisation strength <Math tex="\varepsilon" />.
