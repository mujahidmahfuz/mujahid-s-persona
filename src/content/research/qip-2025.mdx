A generative adversarial network learns to produce data that look like a training set by setting two models against each other: a generator that makes samples, and a discriminator that tries to tell them from the real thing. Quantum GANs move part of that contest onto a quantum computer. Since the first proposals, the idea has grown from theory into experiments on real hardware, spread across many papers. This survey brings that work together.

## The problem

Anyone entering the field meets a scattered literature: QGANs differ in which part is quantum, what data they model, and whether they were simulated or run on a device. Without a map, it is hard to see which designs exist, what they have been used for, and how far they have actually been implemented.

## Method

The paper is a survey. It gives an overview of QGAN models from theoretical proposals to experimental realisations, and organises them by their quantum–classical hybrid structure — which parts of the generator–discriminator pair are quantum. It then summarises where QGANs have been applied, including image synthesis, medical data generation, channel prediction, software defect detection and educational tools.
