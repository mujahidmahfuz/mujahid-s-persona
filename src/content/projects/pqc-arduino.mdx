## Why it exists

Post-quantum cryptography (PQC) is designed to stay secure against quantum computers, but its keys and signatures are large and small microcontrollers have almost no memory. This project asks how far NIST's post-quantum standards can run on an Arduino UNO — 2 KB of RAM, 32 KB of Flash and 1 KB of EEPROM — without leaving the chip.

<Explainer id="pqc-memory-budget" />

## How it fits

A direct port of the reference code fails: the keys alone are bigger than the RAM. What works is a set of memory tricks:

- **Keys in Flash** (PROGMEM) and **ciphertext in EEPROM**, with only three polynomial buffers in RAM.
- **Streaming** — one polynomial or one chunk at a time.
- **Regenerate instead of store** — the matrix and noise are rebuilt from seeds when needed.
- **Reuse** — a polynomial buffer doubles as the Keccak hash state.

## What is still open

ML-DSA (all three levels) and Falcon verification have not been started; the power-interruption evaluation of PQC-Restore is paused. Key generation cannot run on the chip, and the secret key sits in plain Flash — documented as a hardware limit, not hidden.
