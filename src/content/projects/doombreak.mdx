## Why it exists

Short-video feeds are designed so that there is always one more. DoomBreak is an Android app that interrupts the scroll on purpose: it watches how long you have been on distracting apps, warns you with reminders that get firmer, and, if you keep going, takes over the screen — then points you back to goals you set yourself.

- **AI reminders that know your goals.** A FastAPI service on Render calls Groq (Llama 3.3 70B) to write reminders that reference your goals and their deadlines; if the network is down, the app falls back to local text.
- **Usage, measured natively.** Kotlin code reads Android’s usage statistics; the Impact screen shows per-app time with app icons, and daily digests (12 pm, 6 pm, 11 pm) arrive with a pie chart drawn into the notification.
- **One honest number per day — Net Momentum.** `focus minutes + 10 × goals completed − distraction minutes`. It feeds streaks, weekly and monthly trend charts, and a CSV export.

## What was hard

- **The whole app froze — from a widget nobody touched.** After a reminder appeared, every tap anywhere stopped working. Instrumenting the app ruled out a deadlock (a heartbeat timer kept running), a system overlay stealing touches, the notification and the tooltips. Bisecting the UI found the cause: the reminder banner, mounted at the app’s root outside any screen, broke all input as soon as it contained two standard Material buttons — even untouched. The standard fix did not work, so the buttons were replaced with plain gesture-driven ones; the same fix was later needed in the goal picker.
- **Counting usage correctly.** Usage was double-counted until fixed, and an async vibration call crashed the intervention screen.
- **Races in the AI path.** Two reminders could be generated at once; a guard now allows one at a time.
- **Android permissions.** Usage access, overlays and notifications each need a separate grant, so onboarding now explains each permission by what it enables.

## What it does not do yet

It is a prototype in closed testing on Google Play, not a public release, and there is no claim here about its effect on anyone’s screen time. It runs on Android only. The 41 automated tests cover the data models and the Net Momentum formula, app start-up and the reminder banner — not the interventions on a real device.
