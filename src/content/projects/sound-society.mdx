## Why it exists

In many places, people who question the powerful are silenced — by threats, by violence, or simply because no one is listening. Roads break, hospitals fall apart, promises are made and forgotten, and asking about it openly is dangerous. Sound Society is a social website where anyone in a community can raise a problem and ask the people responsible to answer, **without giving their name**. The whole community sees the question, and the office it concerns can answer in public.

It is deliberately narrow: no private messages, no followers, no blocking — it is for public questions, not for fights. The rules say abusive or hateful posts are removed and their authors banned, and a report button sends false or harmful posts to the admins.

- **Anonymous by construction.** Emails and device IDs are stored only as keyed hashes (HMAC with a server secret): the same email always finds the same account and the ban list, but the hash cannot be turned back into the email — and without the key nobody can even test a guess.
- **Moderation before publishing.** Text is checked for phone numbers, emails and long ID numbers (to stop doxxing), threat sentences, and abusive words in English, Bangla and regional dialects — including disguised spellings like “f.u.c.k”, look-alike characters and Bangla digits.
- **Answerable offices.** A *responder* — a union office, a ward member, a journalist — is set by hand and can move a report forward (open → seen → in progress) and reply in the name of that office; only an admin can mark it resolved. Every step is a row on the post’s public timeline.
- **A trust score that only rewards.** Points for truth (+20 when an admin marks a post true), responsibility (+5 for a correct report), kindness and courage (+25 when a problem is solved). Votes from accounts younger than 3 days give nothing, nobody earns from their own vote, and others see only a tier badge.
- **What is urgent.** The “Most Urgent” tab ranks by `(votes + 1) / (hours since posting + 2)^1.2` — Hacker News’ shape with gentler gravity, so an important report in a small community does not sink too fast.

Around it: email, phone and Google sign-in with a remembered device; posts and comments that can be edited and voted on; a profile page and account deletion; search-engine pages (sitemap, robots, titles); and about 510 backend tests. It runs on free tiers only — Render for the app, Supabase for the database and media, Brevo for email, Cloudflare Turnstile against bots.

## What was hard

- **Login codes never arrived.** Render’s free servers could not reliably reach Gmail’s mail port (most tries ended in `OSError`), so codes were lost. Email moved to Brevo over HTTPS, which is never blocked — and codes sent “from” a Gmail address still risk the spam folder until the site has its own domain.
- **Free hosting sleeps.** After 15 minutes without visitors the first request waits about 50 seconds. The app server (Oregon) and the database (Singapore) are also far apart, so every database step is slow.
- **Google sign-in inside in-app browsers** needed a redirect flow instead of a popup.
- **A locked table could hang the database** — updates now give up after 30 seconds instead of freezing the site.
- **The place lists** existed only in pieces; unions created after 2017 and some village lists had to be found, crawled and matched by hand-written tools.

## What it does not do yet

It is a working prototype, not a launched service: there is no claim here about users or impact. Moderation is rule-based (patterns and word lists) — the open-source machine-learning moderation planned in the design is not in yet — and rules can be evaded by new spellings. Anonymity depends on the server secret staying secret and on users not revealing themselves in what they post.

## How it is built

- **Backend** — FastAPI with Pydantic, SQLAlchemy and Alembic on PostgreSQL; per-route rate limits; keyed hashing for identities.
- **Frontend** — React, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS.
- **Deployment** — one Docker image serving both, on free tiers.
